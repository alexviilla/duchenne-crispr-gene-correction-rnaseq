# Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy: CRISPR-Corrected Isogenic Transcriptomics

## Overview

This project analyzes bulk RNA-seq data from a CRISPR-corrected isogenic model of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), to characterize how the absence of a functional dystrophin protein affects the transcriptome of muscle cells across differentiation, and whether the resulting molecular signature can be independently reproduced from public data.

The analysis is based on the public dataset from Morera, Kim, Paredes-Redondo, Nobles et al. (2022), *"CRISPR-mediated correction of skeletal muscle Ca²⁺ handling in a novel DMD patient-derived pluripotent stem cell model"* (Neuromuscular Disorders, [GEO accession GSE189053](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE189053)), generated by Pfizer's Rare Disease Research Unit.

The workflow combines transcript-to-gene aggregation, quality control, PCA, per-timepoint differential expression, and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), across a two-notebook pipeline:

1. **Data loading, quality control and differential expression** (`01_data_exploration.ipynb`)
2. **Functional enrichment analysis** (`02_functional_enrichment.ipynb`)

---

## Research Question

**Does correcting a Duchenne-causing mutation with CRISPR-Cas9 restore a distinct transcriptional program during muscle differentiation, and how does this molecular difference evolve over the course of differentiation?**

---

## Dataset

A DMD patient-derived pluripotent stem cell line carrying the `c.8868delC` mutation in the dystrophin gene (**DMD-K2957fs**) was compared to its **CRISPR-corrected isogenic control** (**CORR-K2957fs**), generated by precisely repairing the same mutation via CRISPR-Cas9-mediated homology-directed repair. Because the two lines are otherwise genetically identical, any transcriptional difference between them can be attributed specifically to the presence or absence of functional dystrophin, rather than to unrelated genetic background variation between individuals.

Both lines were differentiated into skeletal muscle and sampled at **5 time points** during secondary myogenic differentiation:

| Day | Stage |
|---|---|
| -1, 0 | Myogenic progenitor cells |
| 2, 5, 7 | Multinucleated myotubes (increasing maturity) |

**40 samples total:** 2 genotypes × 4 biological replicates × 5 time points.

---

## 01 — Data Loading, Quality Control and Differential Expression

### Transcript-to-gene aggregation

GEO provides this dataset as per-sample Salmon output at the **transcript** level (227,464 transcripts per sample). Transcripts were mapped to gene symbols using `mygene` and aggregated by summing estimated read counts per gene, producing a 44,407-gene × 40-sample count matrix.

**Methodological note:** the original study aggregated transcripts to genes using `tximport` with a static Ensembl release 98 annotation, which additionally corrects for differences in average transcript length between samples. This analysis instead uses a live `mygene` annotation query and simple summation, a reasonable but non-identical simplification. A small fraction of transcripts (~4,600 of 227,464, mostly non-coding or very recently annotated) could not be mapped to a gene symbol and were excluded.

### Quality control

Library sizes ranged from ~5.8M to ~21.5M reads, with 17,255–19,621 genes detected per sample (of 44,407) — a consistent range across all 40 samples, with no sample excluded. CORR samples had a moderately lower mean library size than DMD (~11.1M vs ~15.3M reads, a ~1.4-fold difference), within the normal range of technical variation between sequencing batches and corrected for by DESeq2's internal normalization.

### PCA

*See `results/figures/01_1_pca_genotype_day.png`.*

PCA was performed on the 2,000 most variable genes. **PC1 (50.5% of variance) corresponds to differentiation day**, with samples ordered by time regardless of genotype — the largest source of variation is differentiation itself. **PC2 (12.6% of variance) corresponds to genotype**, with DMD and CORR samples separating consistently across nearly all time points, indicating a genotype-associated transcriptional signature present throughout differentiation.

### Differential expression by time point

Differential expression (DMD vs CORR) was performed independently at each time point using PyDESeq2.

*See `results/figures/01_2_volcano_by_day.png`.*

| Day | Genes with padj < 0.05 |
|---|---:|
| -1 | 3,613 |
| 0 | 2,925 |
| 2 | 4,873 |
| 5 | 4,764 |
| 7 | 5,250 |

The number and magnitude of differentially expressed genes increases from the earliest time points towards Day 7, consistent with the absence of dystrophin having limited transcriptional consequences in early progenitor cells, with effects accumulating as differentiation progresses.

**Design limitation:** each per-day model uses only `genotype` as a design factor; biological replicate was not included as an explicit covariate, which could be a useful refinement given the genotype-associated variation in library size noted above.

### Validation against known calcium-handling genes

The original study identified five calcium-handling genes (**ATP2A1, RYR1, CASQ2, SLN, CACNA1H**) within the leading-edge subset driving repression of the HALLMARK_MYOGENESIS gene set in DMD. As a targeted validation, these five genes were examined directly across all time points in this independent re-analysis.

All five show reduced expression in DMD relative to CORR (negative log2 fold change) at nearly every time point, reproducing the direction of the original finding. However, the pattern is not uniform: **CACNA1H** distinguishes genotypes from the earliest time point (significant even at Day -1, before differentiation begins), while **ATP2A1**, **RYR1** and **CASQ2** only reach clear statistical significance from Day 2 onward, coinciding with the onset of secondary myogenic differentiation. **SLN** shows the weakest and least consistent pattern, reaching clear significance only at Day 7.

## 02 — Functional Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)

Genes were ranked by signed significance (`-log10(p-value)` × direction of fold change) at each time point and tested against the 50 MSigDB Hallmark gene sets using `gseapy`. This mirrors the original study's approach (`fgsea` in R, Hallmark gene sets v6.2) using a different implementation and gene set version — worth noting when comparing results directly, though the overall agreement is strong.

### Reproducing the original findings

At Day 5, this independent analysis reproduces the study's central findings: **Myogenesis** is significantly repressed (NES = -1.83, FDR = 0.0016), while **TNF-alpha Signaling via NF-kB** (NES = 2.41), **Inflammatory Response** (NES = 2.05), **Interferon Gamma Response** (NES = 1.93) and **TGF-beta Signaling** (NES = 1.71) are all significantly activated in DMD — matching the inflammatory and fibrotic pathways highlighted in the original publication.

### Investigating a GSEA warning at Day 7

The Day 7 GSEA run produced a warning about duplicated values in the ranking, coinciding with an unexpected drop in significance for cell-cycle-related pathways (E2F Targets, G2-M Checkpoint) that were strongly repressed at every other time point. To rule out a ranking artifact, the rank metric was recomputed with an explicit tie-breaker and GSEA re-run; the result was nearly identical (e.g. G2-M Checkpoint NES = 0.84 in both cases), confirming that the ~0.01% of duplicated values are a small number of low-information genes unrelated to the pathways in question. **The divergent pattern at Day 7 is therefore a genuine finding, not a ranking artifact.**

### Pathway trajectories across differentiation

*See `results/figures/02_1_hallmark_heatmap.png` and `results/figures/02_2_temporal_trajectories.png`.*

Tracking pathway enrichment across all 5 time points reveals that different biological programs follow **distinct temporal trajectories**:

- **Myogenesis** is repressed consistently and stably across all 5 time points.
- **Inflammatory and interferon-related signaling** (TNF-alpha/NF-kB, Inflammatory Response, Interferon Gamma Response) activate progressively, intensifying towards Day 5.
- **Proliferation-related programs** (E2F Targets, G2-M Checkpoint) show the opposite trend: strongly repressed at Day -1, 0 and 2, then attenuating substantially by Day 5 and 7.

## Key Findings

1. **A CRISPR-corrected isogenic control reveals a genotype-specific transcriptional signature** that is detectable throughout myogenic differentiation (PC2 in the global PCA), independent of the much larger transcriptional changes driven by differentiation itself (PC1).
2. **The transcriptional divergence between DMD and CORR grows over the course of differentiation**, both in the number of differentially expressed genes and in the number and strength of enriched Hallmark pathways.
3. **This independent re-analysis reproduces the original study's central findings**: repression of myogenesis and activation of inflammatory signaling in DMD, using a different transcript-to-gene aggregation method and a different GSEA implementation.
4. **Calcium-handling genes implicated in the original study are recovered, with an important nuance**: their statistical significance and temporal onset differ meaningfully between genes, rather than following a single uniform pattern.
5. **Different biological programs follow opposite temporal trajectories.** Inflammatory signaling accumulates progressively, while an early, strong repression of proliferation-related genes attenuates by later time points — a pattern not evident from a single-time-point analysis, and only visible by explicitly tracking the full differentiation time course.

## Limitations

This project is a computational re-analysis of a public dataset, not new experimental work.

- Transcript-to-gene aggregation used `mygene` with simple count summation, rather than the length-scaled `tximport` aggregation and fixed Ensembl release 98 annotation used in the original study; results are expected to closely match, but not exactly reproduce, the original gene-level counts.
- GSEA was performed using `gseapy` against `MSigDB_Hallmark_2020`, a different implementation and gene set version than the original study's `fgsea` (Hallmark v6.2); pathway names and exact composition may differ slightly between versions.
- Per-day differential expression models included only genotype as a design factor, without an explicit covariate for biological replicate, despite a moderate genotype-associated difference in library size.
- Each time point was analyzed independently; no formal statistical test for genotype × time interaction was performed, so temporal trends described here are based on visual and descriptive comparison across per-day results rather than a single unified model.

## Conclusions

This project demonstrates a complete RNA-seq analysis workflow applied to a CRISPR gene-correction model of a genetic disease, from raw transcript-level quantifications through gene-level aggregation, quality control, differential expression across a differentiation time course, and functional enrichment analysis.

The results show that a single corrected mutation produces a detectable, reproducible transcriptional signature that intensifies over the course of muscle differentiation, and that this signature is not a single uniform process: distinct biological programs (inflammation, proliferation, myogenesis) follow different temporal trajectories in response to the same underlying genetic correction. This independent re-analysis reproduces the central findings of the original study, while illustrating how tracking a full differentiation time course — rather than a single time point — can reveal additional structure in how a gene therapy correction affects cellular behavior over time.
